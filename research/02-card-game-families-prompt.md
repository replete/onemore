# Card game families and their building blocks

Build a structured map of existing card games: the families they fall into, the mechanics each family relies on, and the common variants of well-known games. The aim is to find the reusable building blocks that recur across many games, such as deck types, dealing patterns, ways of playing, and scoring methods.

## Scope

- **Traditional and popular games played with standard decks:**
  - the French-suited 52-card deck, with or without jokers;
  - regional decks where relevant (Spanish, Italian, German and Swiss suits);
  - games popular in the UK and Europe, the US, and elsewhere.
- **Popular games with proprietary decks,** where their mechanics are distinctive (e.g. Uno and other shedding games, Exploding Kittens, Sushi Go). Cover the mechanics only.
- **Collectible and deck-building games, at the level of families.** How do they differ structurally from traditional games? Consider Magic: The Gathering, Hearthstone, Dominion-style deck-builders and living card games.
- **Out of scope:** casino mathematics beyond the basic rules, and gambling regulation.

Pagat.com's classification is a good starting point. BoardGameGeek's taxonomy of mechanics and David Parlett's reference books on card games are also useful.

## Questions

1. **Families.** What's a good way to classify card games by their core mechanic? For example: trick-taking, shedding and climbing, rummy and melding, fishing, comparing and vying, banking games, adding games, patience, collecting. Where do existing classifications disagree?
2. **Each family.** For each family, give:
   - its defining mechanic;
   - 3–6 representative games, with a one-paragraph summary of each game's rules;
   - typical player counts and game length;
   - what information is hidden, and from whom;
   - whether play is turn-based, simultaneous, or allows out-of-turn plays.
3. **Building blocks.** List the primitives that recur across all families:
   - decks and deck changes: stripped decks, jokers, multiple decks, wild cards;
   - dealing patterns;
   - zones: hand, stock, discard, tableau, trick, face-down and face-up cards on the table, kitty/widow;
   - rank orders and trump systems;
   - play structures: tricks, melds, sequences, bidding, betting;
   - scoring and how games end.

   A matrix of families against primitives would be ideal.
4. **Variants.** For the games below, what are the common variants and house rules? Which rules are most often disputed?
   - 21: Blackjack, Pontoon, Vingt-et-un and home versions;
   - Shithead and its relatives (Palace, Karma and others);
   - Crazy Eights, Switch and Mau-Mau;
   - Hearts;
   - Rummy and Gin Rummy;
   - Poker: Texas Hold'em, five-card draw and other common forms;
   - Cheat (also called BS or I Doubt It);
   - Spit, also called Speed;
   - Snap;
   - Go Fish;
   - Whist, Spades and Bridge (briefly).
5. **Casual play.** Which games suit casual groups of 2–8 people, some of whom may not know the rules, and why? Consider how quick they are to learn, how short the rounds are, how much players interact, and how easily someone new can join between rounds.
6. **Speed and reaction games.** Which card games involve simultaneous or speed play, such as Snap, Spit, Slapjack, Spoons or Egyptian Ratscrew? How do their rules resolve ties and simultaneous claims?
7. **Names.** Which game names are generic, and which are trademarks?

## Output

- **Summary:** up to 10 bullets.
- **Classification:** the family scheme, with a summary table of families.
- **Families:** one section per family.
- **Primitives:** the matrix of families against primitives.
- **Variants:** a table for each game listed in question 4, showing each rule, its common options, and where each option is played.
- **Shortlist:** a ranked list of games for casual groups, with the reasons.
- **Sources:** cite them inline with links. Prefer pagat.com, published rulebooks and standard references.
